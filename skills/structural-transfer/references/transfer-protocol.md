# Transfer protocol

Use after deriving the target signature, or for an explicitly requested exploratory transfer.

Describe relationships without domain nouns: “independent actors react to delayed shared feedback, overshoot a capacity limit, and synchronize retries” is a searchable structure; “our system is a brain” is not. Candidate patterns include partial feedback, latent-state inference, redundancy, competition/cooperation, symmetry breaking, conservation, credit assignment, recovery, diversity, phase transitions, distributed decisions, observability, and model mismatch.

Search across distinct mechanisms, not many renamed versions of one idea. Fields such as control, ecology, coding theory, and economics are starting points only. Prefer a structurally better donor wherever found. Record primary source, operating assumptions, and confidence in the source claim. Without retrieval, label donor claims unverified and propose validation rather than fabricated citations.

For every survivor complete this card:

- Source mechanism and evidence.
- Abstract causal principle, including required information and resources.
- Target mapping: source entity/relation → target entity/relation; identify missing counterparts.
- Minimal target intervention and implementation cost.
- Behavioral prediction beyond an aggregate score, including conditions where it should fail.
- Fastest falsification: baseline, manipulation check, observable threshold, and stopping rule.
- Break conditions: incompatible scale, violated invariant, unavailable signal, or changed incentives.

Reject cards with no intervention, no discriminating prediction, or a fatal missing counterpart. Rank survivors by structural fit, test cost, and decision value, not rhetorical novelty. A known method independently rediscovered through transfer may be useful, but is not evidence of a new invention.

For example, randomized contention recovery can map to clients sharing capacity only if clients can vary attempt timing and failed attempts are observable. It cannot recover irreversibly lost information merely because both situations are called “collisions.”
